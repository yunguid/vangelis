# Subwoofer Lullaby opening performance

Original composition: C418 / Daniel Rosenfeld, *Minecraft – Volume Alpha*.
User reference: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Gpd85y_iTxY
Artist: https://c418.org/albums/minecraft-volume-alpha/

Note transcription: OurlioncubMusicBoxes, “Minecraft - Subwoofer Lullaby”,
full-range 60-key arrangement, published January 1, 2018:
https://musicboxmaniacs.com/explore/melody/minecraft-subwoofer-lullaby_19321/
MIDI source:
https://d3nzsgr4mdw5fz.cloudfront.net/media/midi/0/1/19/19321_minecraft-subwoofer-lullaby_aum9_musicboxmaniacs.com.mid
Retrieved September 18, 2026. Composition copyright remains with C418;
this attribution does not assert that the composition is public domain.

The source has 356 notes at 67 BPM with fixed music-box pin durations.
The piano adaptation lowers it one octave, uses 75 BPM (about three minutes),
and shapes velocity in four-bar phrases. The top line (the highest note struck
at a moment, once it clears the accompaniment: A4 and up while the ostinato
runs through bar 20, B3 and up after it) is voiced about 5 dB over the hands
under it; the inner voices sit just under the bass. Note lengths come from a sustain
pedal rather than a fixed cap: the dampers fall on each downbeat that brings a
new bass note (E3 or lower), so a held harmony rings for as long as its bass
does (one bar in the middle section, two in the sparse ones), a repeated key
lifts 80 ms before it is struck again, and the closing notes ring out. The
recorded decay is the only envelope; release is a 0.45 s damper fall, which
is also how the piano gets out from under a takeover note. This is a piano
interpretation of the linked track, not an exact recreation of the original
production. The grand piano is rendered live through the existing sample pool,
MIDI scheduler and reverb, with every pitch at most one semitone from a
recording. The grand is close-miked and dry, so the room is the ambient reverb:
a 3.3 s tail about 13 dB under the piano, low-cut at 167 Hz to keep the bass
out of it. See `public/samples/opening/README.md` for the sample attribution.

The opening plays once per document visit on the home keyboard. Browsers block
audio on a first visit (it starts unprompted only where the site has earned
autoplay, such as the owner's own machine), so while the audio context is not
running a "Turn sound on" button sits under the keyboard. It, or any other
gesture that is not a note, unlocks the engine and the opening begins; on touch
screens the unlock happens on release, which is when browsers grant it. There
are no other opening-performance controls or text. Any
keyboard, pointer/touch or hardware-MIDI note cancels the opening, including
pending asset and audio initialization. Selecting/pasting another MIDI or
arriving with an editor/study handoff also cancels it. Leaving the home route
releases its voices and timers. Returning does not replay a claimed opening.
No song data is requested from YouTube or another external service at runtime.
User patch/session settings are restored at takeover and natural completion.
